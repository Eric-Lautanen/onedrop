## Orientation bug: every barrel and ring on the playfield (user report)

User: *"the bumpers don't line up with the playfield image and they're triangles.
they shouldn' be triangles ... those circles and lights aren't lining up and the
circle bumpers a perpendicular to the playfield instead of laying flat on it."*

**Root cause, systematic.** three.js builds `CylinderGeometry`/`ConeGeometry`
standing along **+Y** and `TorusGeometry` lying in the **XY plane**. This playfield
is **Z-up** (header of playfield.js). So:

- an unrotated barrel lies FLAT on the wood. Seen from the player's view its
  silhouette is two sides meeting in the middle -> **reads as a triangle**, and
  its apparent mass is displaced along Y. That is exactly the bumper complaint.
- a ring already lies flat; giving it `rotation.x = PI/2` stands it
  **perpendicular** to the field. That is the "circles ... perpendicular instead
  of laying flat" complaint.

**Fixed** in `src/js/playfield.js` with two self-documenting helpers next to the
geometry caches:

```js
var UPRIGHT = Math.PI / 2;
function standUp(m) { m.rotation.x = UPRIGHT; return m; }
```

`standUp()` added to: bumper `base`/`skirt`/`body`/`rod`, rail feet `post`,
wireform support `post` + `foot`, tiki `plinth` + `roof` (cone) + 3 `st` stubs,
flipper `boss` + `screw`, post `body`, kickback `coil` + `plunger`, spinner
uprights. `rotation.x = Math.PI/2` **removed** from all four flat rings: bumper
hat `ring`, `plinthRim`, `roofRim`, post rubber `band`. Kept as-is (already
correct): wall `cap` (along the wall), sling `rub` (along the edge), spinner
`top`/`axle` (across X), shack lamp `bar` (across X), brace `post`, ramp mouth
`fl`, plunger `rod`/`knob`, dressing `m` — all use `quaternion.setFromUnitVectors`
or an in-plane `rotation.z`.

Verified: `dev/_bump.js` reports each bumper's solid bbox as **X:Y roundness
0.007** (0 = perfectly round from above) and bbox centre drift **(0.00, 0.00)**
from its layout point.

## Bug found while verifying: `cabinetOpening()` went NaN

`table.js` `cabinetOpening(c)` read `c.sideRail` / `c.endMargin`. `PB.MACHINE_BOX`
is published by machine.js and carries **only** the outer box, so those were
`undefined`, and `2 * undefined` is `NaN` -> `W` and `H` became NaN the moment the
cabinet took over. Every comparison against NaN is false, which **silently
disabled the playfield/cabinet drift guard**. Fixed by defaulting both to 0.

Also: `PF_SIZE` is derived at table.js **script-load**, which is BEFORE machine.js
loads (index.html order: table.js, playfield.js, machine.js, game.js). So the
field is always built from table.js's mirrored `CABINET`; `PB.MACHINE_BOX` cannot
reach it without a load-order change. `checkCabinetFit()` in game.js now compares
what the field was **built** at (`PB.PF_SIZE`) against what the cabinet
**offers** (`cabinetOpening(MACHINE_BOX)`) — comparing `cabinetOpening()` with
itself would always agree and guard nothing.

## Round 3 — "the pink triangles", oversized bumpers, floating wireforms

### Root cause #1: three.js r149 treats hex colours as LINEAR
The one that explains almost everything. `new THREE.Color(0x15191e)` in r149
returns `(0.0824, 0.0980, 0.1176)` — the hex is stored verbatim and treated as
**linear**; there is no sRGB→linear conversion (added in later revisions). The
renderer then re-encodes linear→sRGB on output, so every authored "dark" hex
lands ~1.5 stops too light. Result: the charcoal sling body rendered **mid grey**,
`0x8e1424` rubber rendered **mauve**, and every bit of playfield hardware read as
a pale pink wash. The painted art was never affected because it samples through a
`map`, which IS decoded via `texture.encoding`.

Fix — `srgb(hex)` in `playfield.js` (`new T.Color(hex).convertSRGBToLinear()`),
applied to:
- `phys()` and `std()` (centrally, so STEEL/CHROME/IVORY/WOOD/NEON/... all get it)
- every inline `MeshStandardMaterial` / `MeshPhysicalMaterial` / `MeshBasicMaterial`
  / `SpriteMaterial` colour and emissive (deck, bumper skirt+cap+glow, target face
  + stripe, sling body/rubber/kicker, spinner paddle, kickback coil+glow, lamp rim,
  plunger spring+knob, ramp under-run)
- `new T.PointLight(...)` colours
- the per-instance lamp colours: `tmpCol.setHex(...)` then
  `convertSRGBToLinear()` before `multiplyScalar(v)`, and the same on
  `lampSys.hotLight.color`.

### Root cause #2: `update()` rewrites emissive every frame
Build-time `emissiveIntensity` is only the first frame — `playfield.js`'s
`update()` sets it each tick for bumpers, slings, targets and the spinner. All
the effective base values had to be lowered there, not (only) at build:
- bumper skirt `0.22 → 0.14`, cap `0.35 → 0.18`, glow opacity `0.20 → 0.12`
- drop/standup face `0.30/0.05 → 0.12/0.03`, stripe `0.50 → 0.22`
- sling kicker `0.55 → 0.30`, sling body `0.10 → 0.03`
- spinner `0.28 → 0.18`
ACES at exposure 1.2 clips anything above ~0.35 emissive to white.

### Pop bumpers
`r: 1.10*K` gave a 2.63 u disc against a 1.48 u ball, leaving only ~0.74 u of
daylight to its neighbour — the ball genuinely could not pass. Now `r: 0.55*K`
(1.31 u) and pulled in toward the painted medallions at art 46.2/53.3 (y 29) and
49.2 (y 35.3), giving ~1.70 u and ~2.07 u gaps. The `glow` sprite was
`c.r * 5.4` = 7.1 u across — three of them overlapped into one pale disc that
swallowed the painted medallions and made the cluster look twice its size. Now
`c.r * 2.1` at opacity 0.20.

### Slingshots — rebuilt three times, landed on a leaning capsule
1. Triangle with a red hypotenuse: still read as a flat wedge.
2. Vertical capsule on the painted rail: closer, but too upright.
3. **Final:** a `CapsuleGeometry` bar, top on the painted rail line (art x 13.0 /
   87.0, y 64.9) with the **flipper end swung 2.5 art% inboard** (art x 15.5 /
   84.5, y 75.1). `SLING_LEAN` in table.js is the single knob.
   `slingBar(topX, botX, s)` derives the collision line (the bar's inner face),
   the world normal and the length. The whole bar is one group; its
   `rotation.z = Math.atan2(s.ny, s.nx)` aims the group's **+X at the inward
   normal**, so +X is always "into the field" and +Y always "along the bar" for
   both sides — a plain `atan2(dx,-dy)` does NOT, because the two bars lean
   opposite ways. That makes the rubber and kicker plate sign-free.
   Slings lamps moved to just past each end, following the lean.

### Wireform ramps
They were drawn as a **flat ring at `r.z` on tall vertical posts** while
physics.js already lifted the ball on its own profile
(`b.z = r.z * min(1, min(t,1-t) * 8)`), so the ball appeared to jump onto them.
Added `rampRise(u)` in playfield.js with that exact profile and used it for the
rails, the cross braces, the acrylic deck, the under-run tube and the **post
heights** (posts shorter than `0.35*K` are skipped). Crown heights lowered
`2.4*K/3.5*K → 2.0*K/3.0*K`; `HALF_W` `0.95*K → 0.88*K`.

### Drop targets
`±0.72*K` faces meant the brackets met and the bank read as one continuous pink
bar. Now `±0.56*K` (collision and visual together), bracket `1.62*K → 1.30*K`.

## Round 4 — 3dref-match re-verification + cabinet fixes

The user asked for an independent verification of the cabinet against the

### Measurement gotcha that caused the original pass's worst errors
A naive row profile (`min..max` of the mask) reads the **two legs as one
full-width span**. That is how the original pass concluded the front body was
y 254–610 (356 px, reaching the floor) and placed a "gold lockdown bar" at
y 598–602 — which is actually the **feet**. Always profile rows with **run
structure**. Similarly, the original "body x 30–390 (360 px)" in the side
panel was the **splayed feet**, not the body (real body 49–385, 337 px).

### Scale
`k_front = 27.8/223 = 0.12466 u/px`. The side panel is **horizontally
compressed ~1.21×** relative to the front (legs measure 148 px front / 151 px
side — same number, so the vertical scales agree, but the body's 337 px would
be only 42 u at k_front, shorter than the 48.9 u playfield). So side-view
**horizontals** need `k_side = 0.15030`; verticals use `k_front`.

### Corrections found (all in `dev/3dref-match.md`)
- **Speakers** are **in the lower score band**, vertically centred in it, at
  x 126.5 / 297.5 px (11.4 % / 88.1 % across), **Ø ≈ 30 px = 3.74 u**. The
  original "Ø 75–95 px, overlapping the backglass, 32 % down" was a ~3×
  over-measure — and the previous "fix" implemented it literally, so the live
  speaker cups were the widest thing on the head (bbox 30.51 u).
- **Rails and the lockdown bar are BLACK**, not gold. The original §5 claim
  ("GOLD with a dark strip under the gold") is simply wrong — the yellow
  nearby is the cabinet's palm/sunset flank art.
- **The reference backbox has no gold trim at all** — the bevel rules measure
  neutral grey (54,54,52)/(43,43,43), the top cap is black. The build adds a
  gold top rail, 14 chasing bulbs and gold stiles.
- **Legs**: come out of the cabinet's **corners** (outer face flush with the
  flank), are **19 px = 2.37 u** thick (not 0.84), taper to ~1.87 u, and
  **lean outward at the feet** (~3.1° front / 5.3° side). The original

- Front-view body is y 254–**462** (209 px) and tapers 185 → 203 px; the
